The Educational Gap & What Changes

Fab equipment is too expensive and too hazardous to put in a classroom. Text and 2D image-based lessons flatten complex clean-room processes, and students miss the hands-on anchor that decides whether a STEM career "clicks" or not. A VR station brings all eight major processes inside a headset — safe, repeatable, classroom-ready.

Why semiconductors are hard to teach in-school

  • Fab equipment is too expensive for a classroom.
  • Clean-room chemicals make real hands-on practice unsafe.
  • Text and 2D diagrams hide process flow and equipment scale.
  • Career exploration in STEM lacks a tactile "anchor".

What changes with VR

  • Learners stand inside working equipment, in safe conditions.
  • Process flow becomes 3D, animated and interactive.
  • Schools add a flagship STEM experience without owning a fab.
  • Every learner gets the same procedure — fair assessment.

The 8 Major Semiconductor Processes

Eight process stations — from bare wafer to packaged chip — each delivered as a stand-alone VR module. A learner runs through the full flow in around thirty minutes.

01
Wafer Production
Ingot growth, slicing, lapping, polishing — the starting point of every chip.
02
Oxidation
Thermal oxide growth — the foundation of the gate dielectric.
03
Photo Process
Photoresist coating, exposure & development — printing the circuit pattern.
04
Etching
Selective material removal to define the pattern in the silicon.
05
Deposition · Ion Implant.
Thin-film growth or doping to set electrical properties.
06
Metal Wiring
Interconnect layers that route signals across the chip.
07
EDS
Electrical Die Sorting — probe each die for functional pass.
08
Packaging
Back-grinding, dicing & assembly into the final chip package.
